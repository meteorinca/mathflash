# Grade 4

## Place Value
anytime you need to: read, write, compare, or round large multi-digit numbers by identifying what each position (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) is worth.

## Rounding
anytime you need to: estimate a total quickly, simplify numbers for mental math, or make a reasonable guess without calculating exact amounts.

## Multi-digit Addition
anytime you need to: combine two or more large quantities together to find a total or sum.

## Multi-digit Subtraction
anytime you need to: find how much is left over, find the difference between two quantities, or see how much more one amount is than another.

## Multiplication (1-digit × multi-digit)
anytime you need to: add the same quantity multiple times, scale up an amount, or find the total for identical groups.

## Multiplication (2-digit × 2-digit)
anytime you need to: calculate total items in a grid/array, find total cost for many items, or multiply larger matching groups.

## Long Division
anytime you need to: split a large total into equal smaller groups, share equally among people, or find out how many times a number fits into a larger one.

## Remainders
anytime you need to: handle leftover amounts when sharing equally — decide whether to round up, drop the extra, or report what's left over.

## Factors
anytime you need to: break a number down into equal pairs, find all possible rectangular grid arrangements for a number of items, or simplify fractions.

## Multiples
anytime you need to: find numbers that a given number can multiply into, extend a count-by pattern, or find common meeting times for repeating events.

## LCM (Least Common Multiple)
anytime you need to: find when two repeating events line up together (e.g. buses arriving at the same time, alarm clocks ringing together), or find a common denominator for fractions.

## GCF (Greatest Common Factor)
anytime you need to: divide different items into the largest possible equal sets with NOTHING left over (e.g. making identical gift bags), or simplify a fraction to lowest terms.

## Prime Numbers
anytime you need to: identify numbers that cannot be split into smaller equal groups (other than 1 and itself), used in building prime factorizations.

## Composite Numbers
anytime you need to: check if a quantity can be divided into smaller equal groups or arranged into rectangular arrays.

## Equivalent Fractions
anytime you need to: rename a fraction so it has a different denominator while keeping the exact same value (crucial for adding/subtracting unlike fractions).

## Comparing Fractions
anytime you need to: figure out which portion is larger or smaller, or rank fractions from least to greatest.

## Adding Fractions (like denominators)
anytime you need to: combine parts of the same whole when the pieces are already the same size (same bottom number).

## Subtracting Fractions (like denominators)
anytime you need to: find how much more of a fraction you have, or take away a fractional part when pieces are the same size.

## Mixed Numbers
anytime you need to: express a quantity that is more than 1 whole plus an extra fractional part (e.g., 2 1/2 cups of flour).

## Improper Fractions
anytime you need to: write a fraction where the numerator is bigger than the denominator, making computation (especially multiplication/division) easier.

## Fraction × Whole Number
anytime you need to: take a fraction of a whole group (e.g., 3/4 of 20 students), or add the same fraction multiple times.

## Decimals (tenths)
anytime you need to: measure parts of a whole divided into 10 equal parts (like dimes in a dollar or 0.1 increments).

## Decimals (hundredths)
anytime you need to: deal with exact money amounts (cents), metric measurements (centimeters), or precise data.

## Comparing Decimals
anytime you need to: rank monetary amounts, race times, or measurements from smallest to largest.

## Area
anytime you need to: measure the flat space INSIDE a shape — such as buying carpet, tiling a floor, painting a wall, or planting a garden.

## Perimeter
anytime you need to: measure the total boundary distance AROUND a shape — such as putting up a fence, putting trim around a frame, or running around a track.

## Angles
anytime you need to: measure the amount of turn or corner sharpness between two intersecting lines (sharp acute angles, 90° right corners, wide obtuse angles).

## Lines, Rays & Segments
anytime you need to: describe geometric paths — continuous infinite paths (lines), laser beams with a start point (rays), or measured side lengths (segments).

## Parallel & Perpendicular
anytime you need to: check if two lines never meet (parallel train tracks) or meet at exact 90° corners (perpendicular street intersections).

## Classifying Shapes
anytime you need to: categorize polygons by counting their sides, equal lengths, and types of angles.

## Symmetry
anytime you need to: check if a design can be folded in half so both sides match identically like a mirror reflection.

## Measurement Conversions
anytime you need to: switch between units (e.g. inches to feet, grams to kilograms) to compare or combine different measurements.

## Line Plots
anytime you need to: organize and display frequency of small data points (like lengths of pencils or fraction measurements) along a number line.

## Elapsed Time
anytime you need to: calculate how long an event lasted, or find start/end times for schedules and travel.

## Money Word Problems
anytime you need to: calculate total bill cost, determine change owed, or budget purchases using dollars and cents.

# Grade 5

## Place Value (thousandths)
anytime you need to: work with high-precision measurements, stopwatches, or science data accurate to 3 decimal places.

## Powers of 10
anytime you need to: quickly multiply or divide by 10, 100, 1000 by shifting the decimal point left or right.

## Order of Operations (PEMDAS)
anytime you need to: evaluate a complex expression with multiple operations (parentheses, exponents, multiplication, division, addition, subtraction) in the correct legal order.

## Multi-digit Multiplication
anytime you need to: compute large products like annual costs, population totals, or large area calculations.

## Long Division (2-digit divisors)
anytime you need to: divide large totals among double-digit groups (e.g., sharing 4,500 items among 25 classrooms).

## Adding Decimals
anytime you need to: sum financial transactions, total distances with decimals, or combine decimal measurements.

## Subtracting Decimals
anytime you need to: find change remaining, calculate price discounts, or determine difference in decimal measurements.

## Multiplying Decimals
anytime you need to: calculate tax, find total cost of items bought by weight (e.g. 2.5 lbs at $3.20/lb), or compute area with decimal side lengths.

## Dividing Decimals
anytime you need to: split money evenly among friends, find unit prices, or determine how many small decimal lengths fit into a larger length.

## Adding Fractions (unlike denominators)
anytime you need to: combine fractional parts of different sizes by first finding a common denominator (LCM).

## Subtracting Fractions (unlike denominators)
anytime you need to: find the difference between two fractions with different bottom numbers by converting them to matching denominators first.

## Multiplying Fractions
anytime you need to: find a fraction OF another fraction (e.g., 1/2 of 3/4 of a pizza), or calculate area with fractional dimensions.

## Dividing Fractions
anytime you need to: find how many small fractional servings fit into a given quantity (e.g., how many 1/4 cups are in 3 cups).

## Fractions as Division
anytime you need to: convert a division problem into a fraction or decimal, or share whole items among a larger number of people (e.g., 3 pizzas among 4 kids = 3/4 pizza each).

## Mixed Number Operations
anytime you need to: add, subtract, multiply, or divide recipes or measurements involving whole units plus fractional parts.

## Scaling with Fractions
anytime you need to: predict whether multiplying by a factor will enlarge (factor > 1) or shrink (factor < 1) the original number without calculating.

## Volume
anytime you need to: measure 3D space INSIDE a box, tank, or room — such as filling a box with unit cubes, packing a crate, or calculating water capacity.

## Coordinate Plane
anytime you need to: plot or locate exact positions on a grid using (x, y) coordinates (walk horizontally first, then climb vertically).

## Quadrilaterals
anytime you need to: classify 4-sided shapes based on parallel sides, right angles, and equal side lengths (trapezoids, parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses, squares).

## Triangles
anytime you need to: classify 3-sided shapes by side lengths (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) or by angle types (acute, right, obtuse).

## Metric Conversions
anytime you need to: convert between metric units (kilo-, centi-, milli-) by moving the decimal point based on powers of 10.

## Customary Conversions
anytime you need to: convert standard US units (inches/feet/yards/miles, ounces/pounds, cups/pints/quarts/gallons).

## Line Plots with Fractions
anytime you need to: display and analyze data measured in fractions of an inch or unit (e.g., combining liquid amounts or finding difference between longest and shortest).

## Percentages
anytime you need to: calculate discounts, interest rates, test scores, or express proportions out of 100.

## Numerical Expressions
anytime you need to: translate word problems into mathematical sentences using numbers, operations, and grouping symbols.

## Patterns & Rules
anytime you need to: analyze input-output tables, find the relationship between two number sequences, or predict future terms.

## Interpreting Remainders
anytime you need to: decide what to do with leftover division remainders — round up for extra buses/rooms, ignore leftovers for full packages, or use remainder as exact leftover answer.

## Estimation
anytime you need to: check if a calculated answer makes sense, or quickly approximate calculations when exact precision isn't required.

## Area of Triangles
anytime you need to: find the surface area of triangular shapes using base × height ÷ 2.

## Area of Parallelograms
anytime you need to: find the surface area of slanted 4-sided shapes using base × perpendicular height.
